How households change over time

Turning down the thermostat, buying an EV and installing solar PV can change the amount, timing and source of energy shown on a smart meter. Explore their differences. These sliders are simplified what-if scenarios and are not calibrated forecasts for any real home.

Observed versus expected

To measure an actual change, we need an intervention date, half-hour meter records, historical local weather, enough complete pre/post days, the correct meter boundary and—ideally—verified thermostat, charger or PV instrumentation. The research backend trains on pre-event data and compares a weather-adjusted expectation with post-event measurements, while showing model validation and competing causes.

Important: a lower electricity import after solar installation is not evidence that household appliance demand fell. Extra import after EV adoption does not prove EV charging without a charger meter. A meter trace cannot reveal a precise thermostat setpoint or holiday.

What can we discover from a real household?

With enough data, we can estimate whether consumption changed, how the change evolves over several months, and how much of the difference is explained by weather. Pinning down why it changed requires verified intervention records or additional sensors.
