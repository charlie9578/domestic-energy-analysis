Retrospective weather-proxy backtest

Tomorrow's consumption profile: expected vs observed

Compare a 48-point day-ahead electricity profile with the next day's real meter readings, on exactly the same held-out intervals. ERA5 is historical reanalysis, so this is a perfect-weather hindcast, not an operational forecast. Electricity import is not necessarily total household demand when solar is present.

Backtest your data

Files stay in this browser tab. Require at least 33 complete aligned days; the last 20% form the chronological holdout. There is no random shuffle, and no future meter readings enter prediction.

Inputs and fair comparison

Meter: start_utc,consumption_kwh,duration_minutes,publisher_interpolated (the last column is optional). Weather: start_utc,temperature_c. UTC timestamps must carry Z or an offset.

The temperature regression learns 48 half-hour × weekday/weekend average profiles and a pooled within-slot temperature slope. The calendar-only control uses the same profile with slope zero. Yesterday's zero-order hold copies each of the previous local day's 48 values.

Missing/flagged readings, weather gaps and 23/25-hour local days are excluded: no interpolation or hidden zero-filling. Only the next-day target uses held-out readings. Download ERA5 with the repository weather command.

Next-day preview

This preview is possible only with a complete 48-slot day of separately supplied *future* weather. ERA5 reanalysis cannot provide tomorrow's weather in real time; for live operation replace it with an actual issued weather forecast and record its issue time.

Interpretation and next steps

This is a frozen chronological train/test split, not a rolling re-fit. The weather model is only as good as the weather supplied; hindsight ERA5 may overstate day-ahead skill. Its performance should be compared with yesterday persistence and calendar-only expectations on paired complete days, and later with archived forecast weather at known issue times. Separate gas, whole-home electricity and heat-pump electricity require separate fits and score tables.
